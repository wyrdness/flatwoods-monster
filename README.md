## Overview

The Flatwoods Monster, also called the Braxton County Monster, is one of the most famous American UFO-related entity encounters of the 1950s. On the evening of 12 September 1952, a brilliant meteor was widely observed crossing the skies of Pennsylvania, Maryland, Ohio, and West Virginia. Around 7:15 PM, three boys in Flatwoods, West Virginia (Edward May, Fred May, and Tommy Hyer) saw what they took to be the object descending toward the property of farmer G. Bailey Fisher. Joined by Kathleen May (Edward and Fred's mother), 17-year-old West Virginia National Guardsman Eugene 'Gene' Lemon, and Neil Nunley and Ronnie Shaver, the group climbed Fisher's hill with a flashlight and Lemon's dog. Near the top they reported a pulsating glow, a foul mist that made them nauseous, and, in the beam of their light, a creature roughly 10 feet tall with a 'blood-red' face, glowing greenish-orange eyes, a spade-shaped head behind a black cowl, and clawed hands or appendages. The figure hissed, glided toward them, and the witnesses fled. The story was investigated locally by Sheriff Robert Carr, picked up by Charleston journalists, and given national circulation by Gray Barker in Fate magazine (January 1953) and by the early UFO community. The most-cited skeptical explanation, advanced in detail by Joe Nickell of the Committee for Skeptical Inquiry in 2000, identifies the entity as a barn owl perched in an oak tree, with the cowl and 'body' an artefact of the foliage and the witnesses' panic; the 'UFO' is widely accepted as a meteor that night, with a possible aircraft beacon contributing the pulsing red light. The case remains a foundational episode of West Virginia high-strangeness folklore and is now embodied in the Flatwoods Monster Museum in Sutton (opened 2018) and a series of giant monster-shaped chairs along the 'Braxxie Trail'.

## Theories & Explanations

### Paranormal / Cryptozoological

**Extraterrestrial close encounter** _(Gray Barker, Ivan T. Sanderson, Frank Feschino Jr.)_

Standard early UFO-literature reading: the witnesses encountered a landed extraterrestrial craft and an extraterrestrial entity wearing a suit.

### Scientific

**Meteor + barn-owl misidentification** _(Joe Nickell (Committee for Skeptical Inquiry), Robert Sheaffer)_

The descending object was a meteor (an event independently documented across the eastern US that night). The 'monster' was a startled barn owl perched in the lower branches of an oak above the witnesses, whose foliage formed the silhouette of a body and 'cowl'. Pulsing red light came from a nearby aircraft beacon. Nausea and throat irritation can be attributed to acute fear, the smell of burning ozone or aviation kerosene, and possibly local environmental factors.

### Skeptical

**Mass panic and embellishment** _(Joe Nickell, Robert Sheaffer, Benjamin Radford)_

Skeptical commentators argue the witnesses, primed by extensive 1952 saucer coverage, encountered an ambiguous nocturnal stimulus (likely an owl) and constructed a coherent monster narrative collectively.
